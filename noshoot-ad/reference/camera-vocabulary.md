# CAMERA VOCABULARY — Cinema-Grade Prompting Reference

**Source:** AiKAMI Day 2 Vocabulary PDF (2026-08-18 integration)

Use this vocabulary at the START of every image prompt to control aesthetic signature:
```
Shot on [CAMERA], [LENS], in the style of [DIRECTOR], [REFERENCE FILM] vibe.
```

---

## CAMERA BODIES — Aesthetic Signatures

### ARRI Alexa 65
**Look:** Blockbuster epic, large format, clean, cinematic scale
**Use for:** High-end product reveals, luxury goods, prestige automotive, premium jewelry
**Examples:** "Shot on ARRI Alexa 65" = IMAX-grade production value
**When:** Client ₹1L+ tier, hero shots for portfolio centerpieces

### ARRI Alexa 35
**Look:** Gritty textured realism, modern cinema workhorse
**Use for:** Lifestyle, authentic street/urban aesthetic, raw energy products
**Examples:** Action sports, energy drinks, street fashion, documentary-style people ads
**When:** Realism over polish, kinetic energy, organic feel

### RED Komodo / RED V-Raptor
**Look:** Sharp punchy digital cinema, high contrast, modern aggressive
**Use for:** Tech products, sports performance, automotive (sporty not luxury), energy
**Examples:** Headphones, gaming gear, athletic wear, power tools, performance vehicles
**When:** Digital-native brands, sharp modern aesthetic, under-40 demographic

### Sony Venice 2
**Look:** Clean filmic, beautiful highlight roll-off, natural skin tones
**Use for:** People-heavy ads (finance, service, lifestyle), premium skincare, fashion
**Examples:** HDFC-style actor vignettes, testimonials, family moments, beauty closeups
**When:** Photorealistic humans, soft cinematic without being vintage

### Kodak Vision3 500T (Film Stock)
**Look:** Cinematic tungsten-balanced film grain, warm nostalgic, organic texture
**Use for:** Heritage brands, craft products, retro positioning, emotional storytelling
**Examples:** Artisanal food, legacy watches, bourbon/whiskey, vintage fashion
**When:** Film grain = premium signal, nostalgia, craftsmanship narrative

### 16mm Film
**Look:** Heavy grain, soft focus, retro documentary aesthetic
**Use for:** Indie brands, raw authentic vibe, anti-corporate positioning, UGC-style
**Examples:** Small-batch coffee, vinyl records, zine culture, skate brands
**When:** Intentional lo-fi, countercultural, anti-slick

### 35mm Film
**Look:** Classic cinema grain, balanced texture, timeless filmic quality
**Use for:** Mid-tier premium products, narrative ads, emotional brand stories
**Examples:** Perfume (classic not edgy), leather goods, watches (heritage), spirits
**When:** Filmic prestige without blockbuster budget signal

### 65mm IMAX Film
**Look:** Maximum resolution, epic scale, breathtaking clarity with organic grain
**Use for:** Hero product moments, automotive (luxury/performance), travel, real estate
**Examples:** Supercar reveals, resort destinations, architectural visualization
**When:** "This is the money shot" — absolute maximum production value

### iPhone 15 Pro
**Look:** Natural phone-camera realism, candid modern, UGC aesthetic
**Use for:** TikTok-style ads, Gen Z targeting, "shot on my phone" authenticity
**Examples:** Quick product reviews, BTS content, influencer-style promos, social ads
**When:** Social-first distribution, anti-corporate vibe, relatable not aspirational

## LENSES — Focal Length + Character

### 14mm Ultra-Wide
**Look:** Dramatic scale, strong edge distortion, immersive perspective
**Use for:** Architecture, interiors, environment-dominant shots, epic scale reveals
**When:** "The space is the story" — showcase designed sets, wide product-in-context

### 24mm Wide
**Look:** Dynamic perspective, some distortion, energetic framing
**Use for:** Action, sports, kinetic energy products, POV shots
**When:** Movement, urgency, first-person immersion

### 35mm Standard
**Look:** Natural field of view, balanced, versatile workhorse
**Use for:** General purpose, documentary feel, balanced product-and-environment
**When:** Clean neutral starting point, no specific lens signature needed

### 50mm Normal
**Look:** Human eye perspective, minimal distortion, natural proportions
**Use for:** Lifestyle, people-centric, conversational framing, everyday realism
**When:** Naturalistic, relatable, "what you'd see in person"

### 85mm Portrait
**Look:** Compressed background, creamy shallow bokeh, flattering perspective
**Use for:** Product heroes, beauty closeups, jewelry, watches, perfume bottles
**When:** Isolated subject, background melts to bokeh, premium polish
**MOST COMMON FOR PRODUCT ADS** — our portfolio default

### 100mm Macro
**Look:** Extreme closeup, razor-thin depth of field, texture hero
**Use for:** Craft detail shots, material truth, ingredient closeups, watchmaking
**When:** Beat 4 CRAFT shots, "this is how it's made", quality proof

### 135mm Telephoto
**Look:** Heavy compression, isolated subject, distant perspective
**Use for:** Environmental portraits, distant product placement, compressed layers
**When:** Subject small in frame but visually isolated, layered depth illusion

### Anamorphic Lens
**Look:** 2.39:1 scope aspect, oval bokeh, horizontal lens flares (THE cinema signature)
**Use for:** Cinematic hero shots, automotive, premium tech, luxury positioning
**When:** "This is a movie" — instant blockbuster aesthetic
**CAUTION:** Generates 2.39:1 crops — may need 16:9 reframe for standard ads

### Cooke S4 Prime
**Look:** "The Cooke look" — warm flattering painterly, beautiful skin tones
**Use for:** Beauty, skincare, fashion, people-heavy lifestyle, testimonials
**When:** Flattering human subjects, warm organic premium (not clinical)

### Zeiss Master Prime
**Look:** Clinically sharp, modern precision, cool neutral, perfect geometry
**Use for:** Tech products, watches, automotive (precision engineering), medical
**When:** Sharpness = quality signal, precision craftsmanship, German engineering vibe

## DIRECTOR STYLES — One-Phrase Aesthetic Triggers

### Denis Villeneuve
**Look:** Vast minimal sci-fi grandeur, brutalist architecture, cold beauty, epic scale
**Use for:** Tech products (futuristic), automotive (concept), architecture, luxury minimalism
**Examples:** Tesla Cybertruck aesthetic, minimalist watches, modernist furniture
**When:** Cold minimal epic, sci-fi undertones, stripped-down monumentality

### Roger Deakins (Cinematographer)
**Look:** Master cinematography, perfect natural light, painterly composition, timeless
**Use for:** ANY high-end work — he's the gold standard for "shot beautifully"
**Examples:** Luxury goods, heritage brands, emotional storytelling, prestige anything
**When:** You want unimpeachable craft, no specific style needed beyond "perfect"

### David Fincher
**Look:** Cold precise dark controlled, clinical lighting, obsessive detail, thriller mood
**Use for:** Tech (Apple-style), precision tools, minimalist luxury, crime/thriller vibe
**Examples:** High-end electronics, German automotive, Swiss watches, medical devices
**When:** Precision = brand promise, cool not warm, controlled not organic

### Wong Kar-wai
**Look:** Saturated neon moody romantic dreamy blur, motion blur trails, emotional color
**Use for:** Fashion, perfume, nightlife, urban romance, Gen Z nostalgia
**Examples:** Neon-lit street fashion, club/bar products, indie beauty, vinyl/retro
**When:** Emotion over clarity, saturated color, romantic urban melancholy

### Christopher Nolan
**Look:** IMAX-scale practical realism, epic scope, no CGI aesthetic, grounded grandeur
**Use for:** Automotive (performance), action sports, adventure gear, blockbuster scale
**Examples:** Luxury SUVs, outdoor equipment, men's performance wear, action watches
**When:** "Real not CGI", practical epic, masculine gravitas

### Wes Anderson
**Look:** Symmetrical pastel meticulous whimsical, dollhouse precision, quirky charm
**Use for:** Quirky D2C brands, artisanal goods, indie packaging, playful premium
**Examples:** Boutique hotels, craft sodas, design-forward stationery, whimsical fashion
**When:** Quirky not corporate, pastel palette, symmetry fetish, indie charm

### Alfonso Cuarón
**Look:** Long unbroken takes, immersive realism, natural light, emotional gravity
**Use for:** Documentary-style people ads, social impact, testimonials, journey narratives
**Examples:** NGO campaigns, education products, family services, healthcare
**When:** Realism, humanity, emotional weight, "one continuous moment"

### Quentin Tarantino
**Look:** Punchy saturated retro violent cool, 70s genre aesthetics, kinetic editing
**Use for:** Bold street brands, retro reissues, cult products, anti-establishment
**Examples:** Sneaker culture, vinyl/turntables, hot sauce, punk fashion, craft beer
**When:** Loud bold unapologetic, retro-cool, pop-culture-savvy, edgy humor

## HOW TO USE THIS VOCABULARY

### Step 1: Choose Camera (Product Tier + Aesthetic)
- Portfolio/spec work → ARRI Alexa 65 or Sony Venice 2 (universal premium)
- People-heavy → Sony Venice 2 (best skin)
- Kinetic/sports → RED Komodo (sharp punchy)
- Filmic/emotional → Kodak Vision3 500T (warm grain)
- Social/UGC → iPhone 15 Pro (relatable)

### Step 2: Choose Lens (Shot Type)
- Product hero → 85mm portrait (most common)
- Macro detail → 100mm macro
- Kinetic action → 24mm wide
- People/lifestyle → 50mm normal or Cooke S4 85mm
- Epic scale → anamorphic or 14mm ultra-wide

### Step 3: Choose Director Style (Brand Vibe)
- Luxury/prestige → Roger Deakins (timeless) or David Fincher (precision)
- Tech/minimal → Denis Villeneuve (futuristic) or David Fincher (cold clean)
- Fashion/moody → Wong Kar-wai (saturated romantic)
- Action/kinetic → Christopher Nolan (practical epic) or Mad Max energy
- Quirky/indie → Wes Anderson (whimsical pastel)

### Step 4: Build the Opener
```
Shot on [CAMERA], [LENS], in the style of [DIRECTOR], [FILM REFERENCE] vibe.
```

**Then continue with your existing detailed scene description** (subject, material, lighting, 
palette, texture beats, etc.) — the camera vocabulary is a PREFIX, not a replacement for detail.

## INTEGRATION WITH EXISTING SKILL

**Before (old prompt structure):**
```
Medium product shot of a luxury perfume bottle...
Lens 85mm, f/2.8, shallow depth of field...
Photoreal, commercial product photography, high detail.
```

**After (new prompt structure):**
```
Shot on ARRI Alexa 65, 85mm portrait lens, shot by Roger Deakins.

Medium product shot of a luxury perfume bottle...
[same detailed description as before]...
Photoreal, ARRI Alexa + Master Prime, high-end commercial grade, 8k.
```

**What changed:** 
- Added camera/lens/director opener (controls aesthetic before scene detail)
- Updated finish language from "commercial product photography" to "ARRI Alexa + Master Prime" 
  (matches the camera claim)

**What stayed the same:**
- All existing detail (subject, material, lighting direction, palette, texture beats)
- Prompt length (still LONG & DETAILED for image prompts)
- Motion prompts (still SHORT & PHYSICAL, unchanged)

**The camera vocabulary is an ENHANCEMENT LAYER, not a replacement.**

## COST vs QUALITY TRADE-OFF

**Portfolio work:** Cinema vocabulary + Nano Banana Pro = sufficient quality at speed.
**Client ₹50k-₹1L:** Cinema vocabulary + Soul Cinema (for hero frames) = premium feel.
**Client ₹1L+:** Cinema vocabulary + Seedance 2.5 = broadcast-grade no compromises.

The camera vocabulary improves output quality at EVERY model tier. Even Nano Banana Pro 
responds to "Shot on ARRI Alexa 65" by biasing toward cleaner more cinematic frames.
